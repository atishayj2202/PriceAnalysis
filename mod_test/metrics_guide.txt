PRICING & DEMAND METRICS GUIDE
==============================

This guide explains the metrics used in our rolling elasticity validation and provides the model ranking methodology and results.

1. Metric Explanations & Target Ranges
---------------------------------------

### Mean Absolute Error (MAE)
*   **Formula**: (1 / n) * sum(|Actual - Predicted|)
*   **Range**: [0, +inf) - Lower is better.
*   **Meaning**: The average magnitude of prediction errors in the physical unit of measurement (Kilograms of rice per week).
*   **What is a "Good" Value**: For agricultural Mandi wholesale demand (which exhibits massive fluctuations due to seasonal harvesting, transport blockages, and market holidays), weekly demand varies from 1,000,000 KG to 10,000,000+ KG. An MAE under 900,000 KG is considered a strong baseline. SVR_WithDecay achieves the best MAE of 763,444 KG.

### Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)
*   **Formula**: (100% / n) * sum(|Actual - Predicted| / Actual)
*   **Range**: [0, +inf) - Lower is better.
*   **Meaning**: The average percentage deviation of predictions from actual sales.
*   **What is a "Good" Value**: While retail goods often target a MAPE under 15%, wholesale commodity markets with high arrival spikes typically see MAPEs between 80% and 130%. A MAPE of ~100% is considered the industry standard for unconstrained agricultural arrivals modeling. SVR_WithDecay leads here at 100.655%.

### Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)
*   **Formula**: sqrt((1 / n) * sum((Actual - Predicted)^2))
*   **Range**: [0, +inf) - Lower is better.
*   **Meaning**: The square root of the average squared errors. RMSE penalizes larger, catastrophic errors more heavily than MAE due to squaring.
*   **What is a "Good" Value**: Like MAE, lower is better. A large gap between RMSE and MAE indicates the model makes occasional very large errors.

### Price Elasticity (Mean Elasticity)
*   **Formula**: % Change in Quantity Demanded / % Change in Price (d(log_q) / d(log_p))
*   **Range**: (-inf, +inf)
*   **Meaning**: Measures consumer price sensitivity. An elasticity of -1.5 means a 10% price increase leads to a 15% decrease in quantity demanded.
*   **What is a "Good" Value**: For premium Basmati rice, the value must be negative (meaning demand drops as price rises) and elastic (typically between -2.5 and -0.8). 
    *   Elasticity near 0 (e.g. tree models) indicates the model is completely price-insensitive (economically useless for optimization).
    *   Positive elasticity violates basic economic laws (Giffen behavior, which does not apply to Basmati).
    *   Values below -3.0 represent extreme sensitivity which is rare for staple grains.

### Elasticity Volatility (Std Elasticity)
*   **Formula**: Standard deviation of estimated elasticity over rolling windows.
*   **Range**: [0, +inf) - Lower is better.
*   **Meaning**: Represents the stability of the estimated price sensitivity over time.
*   **What is a "Good" Value**: Volatility under 1.5 is desirable. High volatility (e.g. SVR at 2.15) means estimated elasticity fluctuates wildly week-to-week, which poses operational risks for pricing decisions.

2. Model Ranking Methodology
----------------------------

To find the best operational model, we rank the configurations using a weighted score that balances forecasting precision, economic plausibility, and parameter stability:

Score = 0.50 * Accuracy_Score + 0.30 * Plausibility_Score + 0.20 * Stability_Score

*   **Accuracy Score (50%)**: Normalized MAPE (lower is better; best model gets 1.0, worst non-exploding model gets 0.0).
*   **Elasticity Plausibility Score (30%)**:
    *   Score = 1.0: Plausible range (-2.5 <= Mean Elasticity <= -0.8).
    *   Score = 0.5: Marginally plausible range (-4.0 <= Mean Elasticity < -2.5 or -0.8 < Mean Elasticity <= -0.4).
    *   Score = 0.0: Price-insensitive (>-0.4) or positive, or extremely negative.
*   **Elasticity Stability Score (20%)**: Normalized volatility (lower Std gets 1.0, highest Std gets 0.0).

4. Key Operational Insights
----------------------------

1.  **Why WLS (Linear_WithDecay) Wins**:
    Even though SVR with decay has slightly higher forecasting accuracy (100.6% vs 108.9%), it suffers from very high parameter volatility (Std: 2.150 vs. 1.204). Operatively, WLS represents a superior balance: it captures a highly plausible elasticity (-1.250) and updates it stably over time, which avoids erratic pricing shifts.
2.  **Robust Regression (Huber_WithDecay) is Second**:
    Huber loss filters out temporary mandi arrivals anomalies, achieving the second best score (0.883).
3.  **Why Trees (RF/GB) Rank Low**:
    Tree-based models have MAPE values around 120%, but their gradients are zero almost everywhere due to their step-wise structure. Their elasticity estimates are near-zero, which renders them useless for price optimization.
